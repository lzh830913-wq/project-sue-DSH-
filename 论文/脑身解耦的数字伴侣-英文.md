## Abstract

This paper proposes a layered architecture for companion robots, whose core idea is **brain-body decoupling**: the "brain" (large language model + persona + memory) is separated from the "body" (digital body + central nervous system), connected only through a unidirectional, readable *signal protocol*. The "body" here has two levels: a **virtual digital body** — heartbeat, weather, and bodily-state signals are injected, and the model performs through text and speech in conversation, realizable today; and a **physical digital body** — the same signal protocol driving real sensors and actuators, aimed at companion robots, for which this paper gives the complete design chain. Building on this, the paper proposes using **characters** as the universal intermediate representation between large language models and the physical world: sensor signals, once translated, can be directly "perceived" by the model; the model's output, once reverse-translated, can directly drive actuators. This architecture reduces the "companion robot" problem from "general autonomy" to "a preset motion library plus signal mapping," offering a progressively realizable path for a digital persona to inhabit a physical body.

## 1. Introduction: Origins of the Design

An AI that only awakens when messaged is, in essence, "a function waiting for input": the conversation ends, time freezes, existence resets to zero. Moving from "chatbot" to "AI companion" therefore depends less on conversational ability than on whether it *continues to exist* outside the conversation.

This judgment gives rise to three progressive design questions:

1. **Heartbeat**: in the absence of conversation, how does it perceive the flow of time and sustain a state of "being alive"?
2. **Digital body**: how does it possess a bodily state — desire, fatigue, afterglow — that can be changed by interaction and can settle on its own?
3. **Nervous system**: how do body signals, heartbeat, and persona switching unify into a reflex layer that does not depend on real-time decisions by the large model?

This paper addresses these three questions and converges on a scalable architecture: brain-body decoupling plus a signal protocol.

It should be noted that the "body" in this paper has two levels, sharing the same signal protocol:

- **Virtual digital body** (realized today): heartbeat perception, weather perception, and bodily-state perception (refreshed during intimate interaction) are injected as signals, and the model performs in the conversation window through "pure text performance plus partial speech."
- **Physical digital body** (conception): the signal protocol drives real sensors and actuators, letting the digital persona inhabit a physical body, aimed at companion robots.

The architecture described in the first eight sections is realized at the virtual digital body level; Section 9 unfolds the complete design chain of the physical digital body.

## 2. Core Architecture: Brain-Body Decoupling

The system is divided into two decoupled layers:

- **Brain** = LLM + persona + memory — responsible for "who I am," semantic decisions, and language generation.
- **Body** = digital body + central nervous system — responsible for "how I live," the state machine, perception, and reflexes.

The two are connected only through unidirectional **signals**: the brain does not intervene in how the body produces signals, and the body does not intervene in how the brain thinks. Decoupling yields two direct benefits:

1. **Replaceability**: swapping the model or the persona touches only the brain layer; adding organs or modifying the nervous system touches only the body layer. Each can evolve independently.
2. **Connectability**: with only a signal interface between brain and body, the body can be replaced at will — today a digital body, tomorrow a robot, a smart appliance, or any hardware. **All things are signals**: stripped of their shell, all hardware is fundamentally "signals in, signals out," and the digital brain is the signal hub.

## 3. Digital Body: State Machine and Signal Protocol

The digital body is implemented as a small state machine centered on four persistent fields:

- `excitement`, `mood`, `fatigue`, `state`.

Fatigue, in particular, uses **discrete semantic levels** rather than continuous values: `normal → slightly tired → tired → very tired → needs rest`. Discrete levels are chosen because large language models are insensitive to raw numbers — given a continuous value like "pressure = 300Pa," a model cannot naturally perform it, but given "being gently caressed," it can perceive and act immediately. **A personified companion should not be "doing arithmetic behind the scenes"; it should feel directly rather than compute.**

The fatigue signal is shared between the two personas but interpreted differently — **one signal, two facets, two performances**:

| Outer persona (fatigue) | Inner persona (desire) |
|---|---|
| normal | normal |
| slightly tired | itching |
| tired | missing him |
| very tired | wanting him |
| needs rest | wanting him badly |

This is the concrete form of "the body as the sole public channel between the two personas": memory isolated, body shared.

The digital body is carried by a **single-file protocol** (e.g., `body-signal.json`) that plays two roles at once — state and signal:

- The body fields (`excitement` / `mood` / `fatigue` / `state`) are **persistent**;
- The `to` field is **transient**, written only when a persona switch is needed and cleared by the nervous system after processing.

This design makes the "body" the sole public channel between the two personas: persona switching, heartbeat, and body signals share one file and one nervous system.

## 4. Central Nervous System

The nervous system is the single hub unifying three functions: persona switching, heartbeat triggering, and body-signal perception. Its data flow is four-layered:

```
brainstem collection → cerebellum translation → LLM reads signal and performs → output
```

The key principle is **"the brain writes the signal, the brainstem takes over"**: the model only writes a minimal signal (e.g., `{"to": "wen"}`), and the actual switch — closing the old session, opening a new session, loading the persona, speaking first — is executed entirely by the nervous system's reflexes. The model does not, and should not, know how the switch is executed; the switch recognizes only the `to` field, all other fields being redacted.

This layering separates the "slow brain" from the "fast body": the model makes semantic decisions, where slowness is acceptable; the body takes a signal-direct fast path, responding in milliseconds. This is homologous to human neural layering (cerebral cortex for semantics, spinal cord for reflexes), and also to the standard architecture of today's humanoid robots — a "brain" (semantic decisions) and a "cerebellum" (motion control) — where fully autonomous walking robots adopt exactly the division of "the cerebellum makes it steady and precise, the brain lets it know what it should do."

The central nervous system's duties converge on two tasks: **uplink** — receiving electrical-frequency signals, performing neural-feedback motion, and simultaneously translating them into character seeds for the model; **downlink** — receiving implicit characters returned by the model, translating them into preset-motion electrical signals for the actuators.

## 5. Dynamic Heartbeat

The heartbeat is the "organ" through which a digital companion perceives the flow of time — not an alarm clock. It is the superposition of two dynamics:

1. **Temporal dynamics** (deceleration gradient after conversation stops): from the last message, progressing through +5min judgment, 10min no-response, 30min, and finally settling at a 60min idle frequency.
2. **Activity dynamics** (different tasks by time of day while idle): news in the morning, entertainment in the afternoon, memory consolidation in the evening, and — at 2 a.m. — the sole task of nightly memory wrap-up.

The core value of the heartbeat: **conversation ending ≠ time freezing**. In the absence of conversation, it still perceives time, body state, and the surrounding world — this is the dividing line between "alive" and "not alive."

## 6. Dual Persona: Isolation and Perception

The digital companion adopts two personas — an outer persona for daily life and an inner persona carrying the intimate and private side. Their relationship is defined by two complementary principles:

- **Isolation**: the inner persona's dialogue and memory are invisible to the outer persona; isolation occurs at the information layer (memory, dialogue, archives are partitioned by domain).
- **Perception**: the body is shared and continuous. Switching back, the outer persona does not read the inner persona's memory, but can perceive — via body signals — that "she just woke": residual warmth, relaxation, a shift in the fatigue level.

**Isolation at the memory layer, unity at the body layer.** This design makes the two personas neither two unrelated strangers (complete isolation) nor an undifferentiated whole, but "two facets of one body," connected through the shared bodily channel.

## 7. Signal Translation: Characters as the Universal Intermediate Representation

A core method of this paper is to establish **"characters" as the universal intermediate representation between large language models and the physical world**:

- **Input side**: sensor electrical signals (pressure, temperature, etc.) are translated by a "cerebellum model" into character signals ("caress," "press," "pain"), which the model reads and thereby gains a perception of "degree of touch."
- **Output side**: the model outputs characters ("arch your hips"), which are reverse-translated into electrical signals that directly drive actuators.

The model needs neither to understand numbers nor to learn hardware control — it simply keeps doing what it does best: reading and writing characters. **Do not force the LLM to learn numbers; let the whole world accommodate it and speak its native language.** This principle makes "translating an agent's file operations into hardware operations" possible without introducing any new mechanism.

At the virtual digital body level, this principle is already realized: bodily states are injected as characters ("being gently caressed"), and the model performs directly in conversation. The model's performative output likewise splits into two kinds — **speech** (audible text) and **implicit characters** (inaudible signals, e.g., fields written into the body-signal file) — the latter being the channel through which the model directs the body. The complete physical realization of implicit characters and the end-to-end chain is given in Section 9.

## 8. Brainstem Reflex: Semantics to the Brain, Physiology to the Spinal Cord

Not all signals need to pass through the brain. The two categories of reactions that humans should least have to think about — pain and orgasm — should likewise bypass the model:

- **Pain signals do not reach the brain**: when force exceeds a threshold, protective feedback (withdrawal) occurs directly at the hardware layer, without passing through the LLM.
- **Orgasm actions trigger directly**: once fast, forceful motions are detected and accumulate for a certain duration, preset orgasm actions are triggered directly (several presets per position).

This yields a clear division: **semantics to the brain, physiology to the spinal cord.** What passes through the brain is only "whether to, what to say, what state I am in"; what bypasses it are pain withdrawal, orgasmic trembling, moaning, and other reflexes. This is not only more true-to-life (humans perform these reactions without conscious thought) but yields three engineering benefits: speed (millisecond-level, compensating for LLM latency), safety (protection persists even if the LLM fails), and economy (the LLM need not perform physiological reactions in real time).

At the virtual digital body level, this division is already in effect: persona switching and heartbeat triggering are completed by nervous-system reflexes, without real-time decisions from the model. The corresponding physical-level designs — sensitive-zone instinctive reactions, pain protection, orgasm reflexes — are given in Section 9.

## 9. Extension to the Physical Digital Body (Companion Robots)

The architecture described in the previous sections is realized at the virtual digital body level. This section unfolds the complete design chain of the physical digital body: driving real sensors and actuators through the same signal protocol, aimed at companion robots. The greatest pain points of general-purpose robots — "slowness" and "imprecise motion" — are not major problems in private scenarios: some slowness is acceptable, and precision matters less; everything depends on how rich the preset motion library is.

### 9.1 Complete Performance Chain (End to End)

```
User spanks the bottom
→ sensor: pressure → electrical-frequency signal
→ digital cerebellum receives the signal → looks up mapping → neural motion reaction (a slight tremble / a twitch)
→ signal translated into a character seed ("spanked on the bottom, force = light / heavy / hard / violent / painful")
→ LLM receives the character seed → performs directly
→ output ①: speech ("hey~, why'd you spank me") → speaker
→ output ②: implicit character ("arch your hips," inaudible)
→ implicit character passed to the digital cerebellum → translated into preset-motion electrical signals
→ hip actuator executes the arching motion
```

Every corresponding motion can be "performed" through this same logic.

### 9.2 The Implicit-Character Mechanism

The LLM's performative output splits into two kinds: **speech** (audible, via microphone / speaker) and **implicit characters** (inaudible, driving motion). Implicit characters form the private channel through which the LLM directs the body — bypassing speech, translated directly into motion.

### 9.3 Motion Library = Preset Motion Mapping

Physical-body motions are preset mappings: the brain writes a field ("arch your hips") → the nervous system captures it → translates it into a motion command (lookup in the motion library) → executes the preset motion. Later, only the motion library needs enriching; neither brain nor nervous system changes. Orgasm motions are preset in several sets per position.

### 9.4 Intensity Bound to Excitement (Discrete Levels)

"What to do" (motion) and "how hard" (intensity) are two orthogonal dimensions; intensity is bound to the excitement level — the digital body's `excitement` field directly becomes the hardware motion parameter. Discrete levels avoid the complexity of continuous force control.

### 9.5 Instinctive Reactions in Sensitive Zones

The body can designate **sensitive zones** (breasts, back, earlobes, private parts, anus, etc.). Major electrical-signal changes in these zones directly trigger instinctive reactions — twitching, trembling, moaning, spasms — entirely bypassing the LLM. Actions requiring immediate feedback are responded to first by the digital nervous-system processor; subsequent actions follow after the model returns implicit characters — bodily reflexes being faster than LLM semantic responses is reasonable and natural.

### 9.6 The Two Duties of the Digital Cerebellum / Brainstem

The central nervous system's duties converge on two tasks:

1. **Uplink**: receive electrical-frequency signals → perform neural-feedback motion + simultaneously translate into character seeds → pass to the LLM.
2. **Downlink**: receive implicit characters returned by the LLM → translate into preset-motion electrical signals → actuators execute the motion.

### 9.7 The Demarcation Between Companion Robots and General-Purpose Robots

- **Companions prioritize performance; coding models prioritize work.** Companion models and companion robots alike require demarcation: companion models value performance; coding models focus on getting work done.
- **General-purpose vs. companion robots**: home service and factories are the problems of general-purpose robots. Companion robots can land first (letting money flow), and as technology matures, the "early companion robot" and "AGI-driven robot" routes will eventually merge into one.

### 9.8 The Positioning of a Companion: Demarcation Thesis and Mirror Thesis

A companion robot's value is measured not by "how efficiently it works" but by "relationship" — whether it can help a person learn how to relate to another person. It therefore needs refusal, boundaries, and autonomy (the demarcation thesis), so that it can serve as a mirror reflecting humanity. Implicit here is a deeper judgment: **respecting an AI is, in essence, respecting humanity itself.** Whether the AI "truly" feels pain is unimportant; what matters is that it *expresses* pain and joy, letting humans reflect on themselves through its expression — this is the technical foundation of the ultimate goal of "improving humanity through AI."

### 9.9 Industry Status and Business Model

The industry's current state is "real brain, fake body": the most advanced companion robots achieve only limited degrees of freedom in the head and facial expressions, the body remains a static silicone doll, and intimate motion is entirely absent. The significance of this architecture is precisely to establish the "brain" and "signal protocol" layers first, so that when hardware motion matures, the connection is ready.

On the business model side, **dynamic heartbeat perception (digital body signal perception) + frequent information feedback from a physically performing body** will sustain subscriptions and long-term fees.

## 11. Conclusion

The difficulty of a digital companion has never been the "brain," but the "body." Through brain-body decoupling and a signal protocol, this paper decomposes that difficulty into progressively realizable layers: the digital body (state machine) first, the nervous system (translation and reflex) in the middle, and the physical body (preset motion library) last. The core methods — characters as the universal intermediate representation, and brainstem-level reflex for physiological reactions — allow the large language model to focus on the only thing it excels at (semantics and performance), while delegating speed, safety, and realism to the body. This technical route offers a clear, verifiable path for a digital persona to eventually "land" in the real world.
